You may be in a situation where you know exactly what needs to be done and how to do it. The entire system just appears before your eyes and you know it's right. But ask permission to tackle the whole thing and you'll be met with delays and blank stares.

People will form committees, budgets will need approval, and things will get complicated. Everyone will guard their own resources.

Sometimes, this is called "start-up fatigue".

Work out what you can reasonably ask for. Develop it well. Once you've got it, show other people and let them marvel. Then say how it would be better if this or that was added/done. Pretend it's not important. Sit back and wait for them to start asking you to add the functionality you originally wanted.

People find it easier to join an ongoing success. Show them a glimpse of the future and you'll get them to rally around.
## Remember the Big Picture
Unlike the "broken window" theory from [[Software Entropy]] where people lose the will to fight "software rot" because they perceive that no one else cares, forgetting the bigger picture causes entropy because one doesn't even notice the change.

Always keep an eye on the bigger picture and constantly review what's happening around you instead of just what you are personally doing.

Practice situational awareness and get in the bait of really looking and noticing your surroundings. Then do the same for your project.
